  How the plan works: the skip flow is added on top of 001's reducer without
  changing how loading works. The reload after a failed skip has its own
  actions, because reusing the page load would swap the page for the loading
  skeleton behind the open dialog. The reducer also decides where focus goes
  after each step, so every focus rule gets a unit test without a browser.

  Please check these before I start T1. They're my decisions, not yours yet:
  1. Focus when the reload fails (R9): focus moves to "Check again". The spec
     doesn't say where it goes, and the confirm button that had focus
     disappears. "Check again" is safe because it only reloads.
  2. Button styling: "Keep my order" looks like the primary button, and "Skip
     Dec 16 order" is an outline button of the same size. This is to guard
     against accidental skips.
  3. Wording the spec doesn't set: the dialog heading "Skip your next order?"
     and the amount line "You won't be charged $101.87 for it."
  4. Comparing dates for the "moved date" message: the dates are compared by the
     calendar day the customer saw, not by the raw timestamps. Otherwise a
     time-only change could produce "now Nov 8 (it was Nov 8)".

  Risk I found: in Chrome, a second Esc press without a click in between can
  close the dialog even though R5 blocks Esc while a request is pending. The fix
  is to also block the Esc key press itself while pending, and the e2e test
  presses Esc twice to prove it holds.
